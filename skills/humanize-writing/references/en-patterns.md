# English AI-isms

> Sources: Wikipedia *Signs of AI writing*, GPTZero vocabulary research, community humanizer pattern dictionaries.
>
> Same rule as Chinese: **density, not presence.** One of these words is fine. A paragraph of them is a fingerprint.

## 1. Vocabulary

### Tier 1 — near-certain tells (delete or rewrite)

delve, tapestry, realm, pivotal, testament (to), underscore (verb), meticulous / meticulously, vibrant, intricate, paramount, showcase, leverage, seamless / seamlessly, robust, transformative, game-changer, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, paradigm shift, harness, unlock, elevate, embark (on a journey), navigate (figurative), landscape (figurative), ecosystem (figurative), foster, holistic, multifaceted, nuanced, comprehensive, crucial, vital, utilize, streamline, facilitate, endeavor, myriad, plethora

### Tier 2 — fine alone, suspicious in clusters

effective, efficient, innovative, dynamic, significant, essential, powerful, reliable, scalable, user-friendly, best practices, key takeaways, in today's world, at the end of the day

### Tier 3 — transition spam

Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, Notably, Importantly, Ultimately, Indeed, In essence, That said, It's worth noting, It's important to note, In conclusion, Overall, To sum up

### Openers / closers that scream chatbot

- Openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "In an era where", "Imagine a world where", "Let's dive in", "Great question!"
- Closers: "Hope this helps!", "Let me know if you have any questions", "In conclusion, despite these challenges, the future looks bright"

## 2. Sentence patterns

- **Parallel negation**: "Not X, but Y" / "It's not just X, it's Y" / "This isn't about X; it's about Y". Strongest structural tell. Say Y directly.
- **Rule of three**: three adjectives, three clauses, three benefits. Humans do it occasionally; models do it every paragraph.
- **Rhetorical question + instant answer**: "So what does this mean? It means..."
- **Mirror structure**: "X is more than a tool; it's a mindset." / "More than a product — a philosophy."
- **Dramatic reveal**: "And that's when everything changed."
- **False ranges**: "from casual users to enterprise teams" — the two ends are unrelated filler.
- **Superficial `-ing` analysis**: sentences ending in "highlighting its importance", "underscoring the need for", "showcasing the potential of". AI's favorite way to fake analysis.
- **Em dash overuse** — used as a general-purpose connector. Replace with commas, colons, parentheses, or split the sentence.
- **Uniform sentence length**: every sentence 15–25 words, every paragraph 3–4 sentences. Vary it deliberately.
- **Abstract subject + evaluative predicate**: "The reality of engineering is uglier than these numbers." Say who did what, with what result.

## 3. Structure

- Intro that announces what the piece will do, three tidy body sections, conclusion that recaps — the five-paragraph essay.
- One heading every two or three sentences.
- Bullet lists where prose belongs ("Here are the key takeaways:").
- Every section ends with a mini-summary.
- Symmetrical paragraphs of near-identical length.
- "Despite challenges, X is poised for growth" endings.
- Header-case Title For Every Section.

## 4. Tone

- **Sycophancy**: "Great question", "You're absolutely right", "Certainly!", "Of course!"
- **Over-hedging**: "It's important to note that", "generally speaking", "in many cases", "may potentially"
- **Over-positivity**: everything is "exciting", "amazing", "fascinating", "revolutionary"
- **Fake balance**: "On one hand... on the other hand..." with no actual stance
- **No personal stake**: never "I", never a concrete failure, never "I was wrong"
- **Generic conclusions**: ends with a principle instead of a fact

## 5. Formatting

- Markdown leaking into prose (`**bold**` everywhere, `---` dividers)
- Em dashes, curly quotes, and `…` as single characters (venue-dependent; not proof by itself)
- Tables for two items
- Emoji section headers (💡, 🔥, ✅)
- Numbered frameworks invented on the spot ("The 3-step X method")

## 6. Quick test

Score 1 point each. **3+ points → likely AI draft:**

1. Any Tier-1 word
2. "Not X, but Y" appears
3. Rule of three in more than one paragraph
4. Em dash density > 1 per paragraph
5. Every paragraph ends in a mini-summary
6. Zero concrete numbers, names, or scenes
7. Ending recaps the piece
8. "Despite challenges..." or "the future looks bright"

## 7. Rewriting principles

- Ask "what is this word actually pointing at?" Replace with the fact it gestures toward. If there is no fact, delete the sentence.
- Prefer plain verbs: utilize → use, facilitate → help, endeavor → try, leverage → use.
- Keep repetition when it is the clearest word (elegant variation is an AI habit, not a virtue).
- Let one thought stay unresolved; not every paragraph needs a landing.
